When the air conditioning quits in Savannah, GA, the house tells you fast, and so does everyone in it. The most common failure we see is the humble run capacitor: a few dollars of electronics that starts the compressor and fan, weakens quietly for years, and finally lets go on a brutal afternoon, leaving the outdoor unit humming but the fan still. Right behind it come pitted contactors that no longer pass power cleanly, condenser fan motors with dry bearings, refrigerant leaks that slowly bleed the system's capacity away, and evaporator coils that ice over solid whenever airflow collapses behind a clogged filter.

At Savannah HVAC, every AC repair starts with measurement. Capacitors are metered against the rating on the label. Amp draws on the compressor and fan motor are compared to the data plate. Refrigerant charge is judged by superheat and subcooling, not by pressure readings alone, and any icing prompts a look at the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter. This matters because a single warm-air symptom can hide four different faults at four very different prices, and a refrigerant top-off that ignores the leak is just a short-term rental on cold air.

Honest advice is part of every visit. If an aging system's compressor has failed, we explain the repair-versus-replacement math plainly and let you decide. Most of the time, though, the fix is simpler, and because our trucks stock capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, cooling is usually restored before we leave. Signs You Need AC Repair in Savannah

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Puddles near the air handler or water stains on the ceiling beneath it
  • Rising electric costs paired with a sticky, uncomfortable house
  • Ice on the refrigerant lines or a frozen indoor coil
  • A condenser fan that sits still while the outdoor unit hums
  • Breaker trips or a burning odor at startup
  • Rapid on-off cycling that never brings the temperature down
  • Hissing or bubbling sounds that suggest a refrigerant leak
  • Vents blowing air that is barely cooler than the room

Why Professional AC Repair Matters

Start with the refrigerant. It lives in a sealed, pressurized loop, venting it is prohibited under federal rules, and correct service requires gauges, a vacuum pump, and a recovery machine rather than a store-bought recharge kit. Add capacitors that hold a dangerous charge with the power off and a condensing unit fed by 240 volts, and the risks add up quickly. Then there are the diagnostic traps: an iced coil often points to poor airflow rather than low charge, and topping off a system with an unrepaired leak lets moisture and acid slowly damage the compressor while your money leaks out with the refrigerant.

Professional repair gets the whole picture. Our experienced technicians meter electrical components instead of swapping them, weigh charge decisions against measured superheat and subcooling, find leaks with detectors and dye rather than assumptions, and check the filter, blower, and duct static pressure whenever airflow is suspect. That is the difference between a repair that holds through the season and a parts-cannon fix that calls you back in three weeks. And when the system genuinely is not worth saving, we tell you that plainly too, with numbers instead of pressure.

AC Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • A new buzz or click at startup often means a weak capacitor, so call before it fails
  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Book a professional tune-up each spring before the first heat wave finds the weak parts
  • Keep shrubs two feet from the outdoor unit and gently rinse its coil each spring
  • Shade is fine, but do not box in the outdoor unit with fences or covers
  • Change the filter monthly during cooling season; starved airflow is how coils freeze
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
