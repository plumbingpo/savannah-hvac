Think about the spaces your central system has always struggled with: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs hot all summer, or an older Savannah home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the spaces a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a three-inch hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor modulates to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it works as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Most disappointing mini splits were doomed on paper, not on the wall. The most common error is oversizing: a head too big for its room cools in a hurry, shuts off before it pulls out the humidity, and hunts all day. Savannah HVAC sizes every zone to its real load instead. On multi-zone systems we also check that the outdoor unit's capacity makes sense across the heads it feeds and that every line set stays within the manufacturer's length and lift limits. Then comes placement. We walk the Savannah home with you, weigh wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor-console options, and put each head where it throws air where you live and still looks right, with outdoor line sets dressed in line-hide rather than left bare.

Installation gets the same rigor as our largest systems. The line set is pressure-tested with nitrogen and evacuated to a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, and flare connections are torqued and leak-checked, since flare joints are where careless installs leak. Condensate is routed to drain by gravity or a reliable pump, and the charge is adjusted for line set length per the manufacturer's tables. We commission every zone in both heating and cooling, then hand you the remotes with the settings explained in plain terms. Why Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation Matters

Most failed mini splits we see in Savannah trace back to installs that treated the job as simple. Refrigerant leaks at the flares are the headline problem: a joint that was poorly formed or never torqued to spec leaks slowly, and the homeowner finds out a season later when the head stops cooling. Moisture is the next issue, because an install that skips the nitrogen pressure test and a micron-verified vacuum leaves contamination inside a system whose inverter compressor is sensitive to it. Overlong or overly tall line set runs steal capacity quietly, and a drain line without enough fall will drip down the wall as soon as humidity climbs.

Here is the part most installers underestimate: sizing. Even the smallest indoor heads are oversized for plenty of bedrooms, and an oversized head cycles constantly, leaves moisture in the air, and never finds a steady state. Savannah HVAC sizes zones honestly and installs them with the same care, torquing and testing every flare, proving the line set with nitrogen, pulling a measured vacuum, adjusting charge for the run, and commissioning each head in both modes. The refrigerant side is federally regulated and the electrical side calls for a properly sized circuit and disconnect, so neither is a weekend project. What you get is a system that stays silent and efficient for fifteen years instead of a rushed install that never quite works.

Ductless Mini Split Installation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Rinse or wash the indoor unit's filter screens monthly; they load up faster than furnace filters
  • Check the condensate outlet during cooling season to make sure water is actually coming out
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • Choose a setpoint and leave it alone, since inverters run best without constant on and off switching
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Give the outdoor unit open space in every season, free of drifts, debris, and mowing clippings
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
