No Surprises

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we touch a tool, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and tonnage of your system, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get a straightforward recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

The problem with HVAC failures is that many of them develop out of sight. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil slowly drains refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down gradually, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Many Savannah homeowners call us to size up an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new unit during a cold snap. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Preventive Care

Tune-Ups That Keep Your System Running

A Savannah HVAC maintenance visit is more than a filter change. Our technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, replaces the filter, and checks static pressure in the duct system. Minor faults are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your system stays running at full efficiency.

Many homes do best with two visits a year: a spring tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and local conditions such as dust and pollen, which clog condenser coils and raise energy use fast.

The savings show up faster than most people expect. Clean coils move heat more efficiently, a correct refrigerant charge keeps the compressor from overworking, and a fresh filter spares the blower motor. Compared with the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Right-Sized Systems

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

Getting the size right is one of the most overlooked parts of any install. Before quoting new equipment, Savannah HVAC measures the details that drive your home's heating and cooling load: square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout. That gives you a system that runs in long, efficient cycles, keeps humidity in check, and holds every room at the temperature you set.

Homes change over time. An addition or a finished basement can overwhelm the single-stage system that once kept up just fine. Today's two-stage and variable-speed equipment holds tighter temperatures and uses much less energy. If you're torn between a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll explain the real trade-offs in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Savannah

The majority of repair calls we take in Savannah come down to a handful of familiar problems. The AC runs but the air never gets cold. The furnace won't light, or it lights and shuts off minutes later. Ice builds up on the evaporator coil, the outdoor unit hums but never kicks on, or a mini-split starts flashing error codes. Add refrigerant leaks, clogged drains, and forgetful thermostats, and you have the full list. Our technicians handle all of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

Not every HVAC problem is equally urgent. A weak capacitor just keeps the compressor from starting. A cracked heat exchanger, on the other hand, can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a backed-up condensate drain can soak through a ceiling before anyone notices. We come prepared to replace capacitors, contactors, fan motors, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards, seal ductwork that's leaking air into the attic, and recharge refrigerant once a leak is fixed.
