How to Spot AC Trouble Early in Savannah, GA

Home / Blog / How to Spot AC Trouble Early in Savannah, GA

As the warmest months approach here in Savannah, GA, our air conditioners become critical components of our home comfort. Your AC unit works overtime to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners aren't designed to last forever and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a major repair or replacement might be on the horizon. Recognizing these warning signs early can save you from a sudden and uncomfortable breakdown on the hottest day of the year, and potentially minimize damage in the long run. Here at Savannah HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: Feeling Less Cool Than Before?

One of the most common and immediate signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a drop in its cooling capability. You may constantly be tweaking the thermostat down only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Uneven cooling throughout your house is a tell-tale sign. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. This could stem from various underlying issues. It could be a low refrigerant level, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. It's the critical component for heat exchange. Another common culprit is a dirty air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. If the evaporator coils are fouled, cooling suffers, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. Even a failing compressor, the heart of your AC, could be the cause. If you're experiencing poor temperature control, it's time to have a professional from Savannah HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: When Your AC Sounds Off

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing unexpected auditory disturbances coming from your unit, it's a clear indicator that something is amiss. Worn motor bearings are often the source of grinding or squealing sounds. These bearings, key to rotational movement, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Repetitive clicking might signal a faulty relay or other electrical trouble. Loud banging or clunking is especially worrisome, as they might suggest a major mechanical failure within the compressor or fan. These types of sounds should never be ignored. Addressing these auditory clues promptly can prevent a small issue from becoming a costly repair.

Increased Energy Bills: Why Is My Electric Bill So High?

Your electric bill mysteriously climbing is a common indicator of AC troubles. When your energy consumption hasn't changed but your bills are spiking, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. Several issues can lead to this lack of efficiency. As mentioned, a dirty filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. Ductwork leaks let valuable cooled air escape into areas that don't need it, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. Units that are too old or not properly sized for your home are inherently less efficient. Refrigerant leaks also force the system to work harder. Over time, components like the compressor and fan motors can lose their efficiency. When your energy bills are unexpectedly high, it's worth having Savannah HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. We can help you understand if a repair or a more efficient replacement makes sense.

Poor Airflow: Is Air Barely Coming Out of the Vents?

Proper airflow ensures consistent cooling and even temperatures across all rooms. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have a mere whisper of air than others, it's a clear warning of an underlying problem. Poor airflow can be attributed to multiple factors. A very common culprit is a dirty or clogged air filter, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Blocked return air vents can also hinder airflow. The fan motor, if failing, is another source of poor airflow. A struggling fan motor won't adequately circulate air through your home's ducts. Obstructions in the ductwork, such as debris or even collapsed sections, can also be a factor. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. Addressing poor airflow is important not just for comfort, but also because it forces your AC to work harder, potentially shortening its lifespan.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Is Your AC Leaking Water?

Some condensation is expected from an AC, but significant moisture or standing water around the unit is problematic. This is a clear red flag that something is wrong. The most common cause of water leaks is a clogged condensate drain line. The AC dehumidifies, sending collected water through a condensate line. If this line becomes blocked by algae, dirt, or debris, the water will back up and overflow the pan. This can result in damage to your property and even pose electrical risks. Leaks can also originate from a cracked or rusted drain pan. If there's a major refrigerant leak, ice can build up on the coils, melt, and cause overflow. If you see unexpected wetness around your system, it's imperative to address it quickly to prevent further damage. Savannah HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: Is Your AC Turning On and Off Too Often?

Normal AC operation involves consistent cycles of cooling and then resting. If you notice your AC unit turning on and off much more frequently than usual, this is known as "short cycling" and it's a significant warning sign. Short cycling means your AC isn't completing a full cooling cycle, which is inefficient and puts undue stress on the system. There are various potential causes for this behavior. If your AC is too big for your home, it might short cycle, cooling fast but not dehumidifying. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. Electrical problems or a failing compressor can also cause short cycling. Whatever the reason, frequent short cycling accelerates wear on AC components, resulting in earlier breakdowns and higher energy use. An AC that short cycles isn't working properly and requires professional diagnosis.

Old Age: The Inevitable March of Time for Your AC

Even without obvious problems, your AC's age is a key indicator of its approaching end-of-life. Typically, central AC units last 10-15 years, with exceptional cases reaching 20 with good maintenance. As your unit approaches or exceeds this age range, it becomes more prone to breakdowns and less efficient. Older units may rely on R-22 refrigerant, which is phased out and costly to maintain due to scarcity. Components in older systems are naturally more worn from years of use. While regular maintenance can certainly extend the life of your AC, there comes a point where repairs become more frequent and costly than investing in a new, more efficient system. If your AC unit is over 10 years old and you're noticing any of the other signs mentioned above, it's a good idea to start planning for a replacement. A modern, efficient model can provide substantial energy savings and enhanced comfort for many years.
